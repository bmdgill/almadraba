3 to 13 May 2026

Seville, Conil and almadraba tuna

A flexible food-first trip plan built around Seville architecture, Conil as the coastal base, tuna season in Conil, Barbate and Zahara, and a final Cadiz option that adds variety without making the trip feel busy.

Trip brief

Copyable context for future planning

Known bookings

  • Outbound: London Luton to Seville, Sunday 3 May, 16:05.
  • Seville: 3 nights, 3 to 6 May, Sercotel Don Luciano.
  • Conil: 4 nights, 6 to 10 May, Alzocaire Hostal Boutique.
  • Inbound: Seville to London Luton, Wednesday 13 May, 11:05.
  • Car: from leaving Seville until returning to Seville airport.

Preferences

  • Tuna is a major motivation, especially wild red tuna from almadraba season.
  • Restaurants should feel trendy, unique or genuinely special. Value matters more than low price.
  • No dietary restrictions.
  • Active but not hectic: beautiful walks, coast, nature, history, architecture and beaches.
  • Prefer flexibility and minimal booking, except where booking unlocks something clearly worthwhile.
  • Day trips up to 1 to 1.5 hours are acceptable, but only once or twice.

Planning principle

Book the scarce things, keep everything else modular. The scarce things are the best tuna meals, the Alcazar if you want it, Conil parking, and a cancellable Cadiz base for 10 to 13 May.

Recommended shape

The strongest plan is Seville, Conil, then Cadiz

Best balance: Cadiz 10 to 13 May

This is the recommendation. Four nights in Conil gives you enough time for the town, the beach, Roche, La Brena, Barbate, and the opening of Conil's tuna route. Moving to Cadiz for the final block adds a walkable city, better rainy or windy day options, strong tapas, and an easier psychological final run to Seville airport.

Action: book a cancellable Cadiz old-town or La Vina hotel with parking or a named garage nearby. Reassess on 8 May.

Simplest: stay in Conil 10 to 13 May

This is still a defensible choice if you want the fewest logistics and fall in love with the Conil rhythm. It gives more time for the Conil tuna route, Zahara's route opening on 12 May, and beach days. The risk is that the trip becomes one coastal mode for too long.

Most conservative: final airport night

Use this only if the 11:05 flight feels stressful. It removes the early drive, but costs you the last proper evening on the coast or in Cadiz. For most travellers, Cadiz to Seville airport with an early departure is reasonable.

Book or confirm first

  1. Cancellable Cadiz stay, 10 to 13 May, with parking plan.

  4. One special Seville dinner: Canabota, La Barra de Canabota, Manzil or Sobretablas.
  5. Cooking Almadraba in Conil for 6, 7 or 8 May.
  6. El Campero lunch in Barbate for 9 or 10 May.

High-level itinerary

Day by day, with flexible modules

The plan uses active mornings, long lunches, low-pressure afternoons and sunset walks. Where the weather, wind or mood changes, swap the named activity for another card in the activities section.

Sun3 May

Arrive in Seville

Land in the evening after the 16:05 Luton flight. Take taxi or the EA airport bus into town, check in, then keep dinner light. Do not force a destination meal on arrival night.

Food: Eslava or a simple central tapas fallback if energy allows. Walk: Alameda or the river if you want air after travel.

Mon4 May

Seville's highest-value core

Start with the Alcazar if you booked an early slot. Treat it as palace, garden and water engineering, not a box-tick monument. Use the hottest part of the day for lunch and shade. Later, drift through Santa Cruz, the Cathedral exterior and the river.

Backup if you skip the Alcazar: Casa de Pilatos plus Palacio de las Duenas. Dinner should stay casual unless your top restaurant choice has Monday availability.

Tue5 May

Palaces, Triana and one special dinner

Use the morning for Casa de Pilatos or Duenas, both more intimate and less draining than another giant sight. Cross to Triana for ceramics, river light and a slower late afternoon.

Dinner: Canabota or La Barra de Canabota for seafood precision, Manzil for a more contemporary Andalusian angle, or Sobretablas if you want modern but softer in mood.

Wed6 May

Pick up the car and move to Conil

Leave Seville without a rushed morning. Pick up the car, drive south, check in at Alzocaire, and make the first coastal evening simple: Los Bateles, Fuente del Gallo or El Roqueo at sunset.

Dinner: Cooking Almadraba if you secured it, otherwise Feduchy or a beach seafood dinner at La Fontanilla.

Thu7 May

Conil and Calas de Roche

Walk the Calas de Roche trail while it is cool. It gives cliffs, coves and sea colour without a long drive. Spend the afternoon exactly as conditions suggest: beach, old town, or a long lunch.

Food: La Fontanilla or Francisco Fontanilla for classic beach seafood. Ask what tuna cuts are best that day.

Fri8 May

Conil Ruta del Atun opens

This is the first day of Conil's 2026 tuna route. Keep the day light so you can sample the route without feeling over-scheduled. Morning beach at El Palmar or Los Bateles, then tuna tapas in town from late afternoon.

Decision point: if Conil feels perfect and weather looks good, consider extending. If you want variety, keep the Cadiz plan.

Sat9 May

La Brena cliffs and El Campero

This is the strongest active-food pairing of the coastal stay. Walk La Brena to Torre del Tajo in the morning, then go to Barbate for El Campero. After a serious tuna lunch, finish with Cabo Trafalgar or Zahora rather than another formal dinner.

Book lunch. Order across raw, cured, grilled and fatty cuts if the kitchen can guide you.

Sun10 May

Move to Cadiz via Vejer

Check out of Conil and make Vejer the elegant transfer stop: white lanes, views, architecture and a memorable lunch. Continue to Cadiz late afternoon, park the car, then do not move it again unless needed.

Food: Jardin del Califa for atmosphere or La Castilleria for a palate-reset meat lunch. If you stay in Conil, keep Vejer as a day trip instead.

Mon11 May

Cadiz as a walking city

Use Cadiz for what it does best: old-town wandering, sea walls, La Vina, El Populo, the Roman theatre, Cathedral views and a slow tapas day. This is your low-driving recovery day.

Food: Casa Manteca for institution energy, La Tabernita for a tiny tapas crawl, and market snacks if you want less structure.

Tue12 May

Choose Zahara tuna or Bolonia history

Zahara's 2026 tuna route begins today, which makes it the more season-specific choice. Go for Antonio or Zokarra, Atlanterra beach and a route tasting. If you want one bigger landscape and archaeology day, choose Bolonia and Baelo Claudia instead.

Do not try to do Zahara, Bolonia and Tarifa properly in one day. Pick the one that matches the weather and appetite.

Wed13 May

Return to Seville airport

For an 11:05 flight, leave Cadiz around 06:45 to 07:00, earlier if you still need fuel or have a slow rental return. From Conil, leave closer to 06:20 to 06:30. Keep breakfast, fuel and toll-free route decisions settled the night before.

Food deep dive

Tuna season, restaurants and what to order

Why the timing matters

The trip overlaps the most useful part of the almadraba calendar, but not evenly across towns. Barbate's 2026 tuna week ends on 3 May, so it is effectively missed because you fly that afternoon. Conil's Ruta Gastronomica del Atun runs 8 May to 8 June, which lands perfectly in your Conil stay. Zahara de los Atunes starts its 2026 route on 12 May, making your final full day the ideal moment for a Zahara tuna day if you want one more season-specific push.

The best strategy is not to chase every event. Use Conil for the opening weekend, use Barbate for El Campero, and use Zahara only if the 12 May opening day still sounds fun after several tuna meals.

Tuna order strategy

Ask for "atun rojo salvaje de almadraba" and let the restaurant guide cuts. Aim for contrast: one raw or lightly cured plate, one lean grilled or sauced cut, one fatty cut, and one traditional preparation. Useful cut names include lomo, tarantelo, descargamento, ventresca, morrillo, contramormo, parpatana and galete. If a restaurant offers a tasting or cut-led sequence, take it once rather than improvising.

Canabota or La Barra de Canabota Seville seafood splurge

Canabota is the Seville seafood booking if you want the first part of the trip to feel special without pretending Seville is the tuna coast. It is about product, fish handling and precise cooking. La Barra is the more relaxed counter-style sibling and may fit better if you want excellence without a long tasting-menu mood.

Why it fits: trendy, serious, product-led and memorable. Caveat: it is not the best value if the real goal is almadraba tuna, so do it only once, preferably on 5 May.

Booking level: high.

Manzil Contemporary Andalusian dinner

Manzil is the Seville alternative if you want a restaurant that feels modern, designed and rooted in Andalusian references rather than a pure seafood counter. It is useful because it diversifies the food story before several tuna-heavy coastal days.

Why it fits: unique and city-specific. Caveat: choose this over Canabota only if seafood repetition worries you.

Booking level: medium to high.

Sobretablas Modern, softer Seville meal

Sobretablas is a good value-oriented special meal: contemporary Andalusian cooking without the sharp expense or theatre of the top seafood option. It works well for a relaxed final Seville night or a long lunch before driving south if opening times fit.

Why it fits: good balance of interest, comfort and value. Caveat: not a tuna-season restaurant, so keep it as a Seville choice only.

Booking level: medium.

Eslava Polished tapas fallback

Eslava is useful as a low-friction Seville meal: polished tapas, central enough, lively, and less of a commitment than a major dinner. It is a good arrival-night or Monday option if you want something reliable without building the day around it.

Why it fits: high satisfaction for low planning. Caveat: popular, so go off-peak or be prepared to pivot.

Booking level: low to medium.

Cooking Almadraba Best Conil tuna booking

This is the Conil restaurant that most directly matches the trip theme. It is small, focused and Michelin-listed, with the right product story for a town that is starting its tuna route on 8 May. Use it for your first serious Conil dinner, ideally 6, 7 or 8 May.

Why it fits: season, place and product all line up. Caveat: small restaurants can be hard to book and spring hours can shift.

Booking level: high.

La Fontanilla Classic beach seafood lunch

La Fontanilla is a strong lunch choice because it places the food in the right physical setting: sand, sea and Conil's seafood identity. It is not the trendiest option, but it is exactly the kind of traditional beach restaurant that makes a coastal trip feel grounded.

Why it fits: beach rhythm, seafood and tuna without a big drive. Caveat: choose lunch over dinner for light and atmosphere.

Booking level: medium.

Francisco Fontanilla Neighbouring beach classic

Francisco Fontanilla is another useful classic in the same beach ecosystem. It gives you an easy fallback if La Fontanilla is full or if one menu looks stronger on the day. Use it for fried fish, rice, shellfish and whatever tuna looks freshest.

Why it fits: flexible, local, low admin. Caveat: less distinctive than Cooking Almadraba or El Campero.

Booking level: medium.

Feduchy Trendy Conil old-town option

Feduchy is useful when you want a more contemporary Conil dinner that is not another beach seafood institution. It gives variety, cocktails or wine-bar energy, and a flexible old-town location.

Why it fits: trendy, accessible and good for nights when you do not want a formal tuna meal. Caveat: do not let it replace the destination tuna meals.

Booking level: low to medium.

El Campero The canonical tuna meal

El Campero is the clearest must-book meal of the trip. Barbate is one of the tuna capitals of the coast, and this restaurant is built around red tuna in many forms: raw, cured, grilled, stewed and cut-led. Pair it with La Brena in the morning and make lunch the main event.

Why it fits: if you came for tuna, this is the benchmark. Caveat: Barbate's official 2026 tuna week ends the day you fly, so the restaurant matters more than the town event for your dates.

Booking level: very high.

Antonio Zahara classic

Antonio is the classic Zahara red-tuna address and fits best on 12 May, when Zahara's 2026 route begins. It is more old-school than trendy, but it has the confidence of a place deeply associated with the product and beach setting.

Why it fits: Zahara plus route opening plus tuna expertise. Caveat: opening days and spring schedules are worth confirming directly.

Booking level: high.

Zokarra More modern Zahara tuna option

Zokarra is the Zahara choice if you want the day to feel a little more contemporary. It is useful as a backup to Antonio or as the primary choice if the menu looks sharper for grilled fish, tuna and a younger dining room.

Why it fits: trendier feel in the right tuna town. Caveat: if you want the classic institution, Antonio has the stronger identity.

Booking level: medium to high.

Jardin del Califa Atmospheric Vejer lunch

Jardin del Califa is not a tuna restaurant, which is part of its usefulness. It adds North African-Andalusian mood, courtyards and a sense of place during the Conil-to-Cadiz transfer. It works best when you want Vejer to be a memorable pause rather than just a viewpoint stop.

Why it fits: unique, romantic and logistically clean. Caveat: reserve if you want the courtyard experience.

Booking level: medium.

La Castilleria Palate reset near Vejer

La Castilleria is a deliberate non-tuna recommendation. After several seafood days, a serious grilled-meat lunch in Santa Lucia can make the whole trip feel better balanced. It pairs naturally with Vejer if you are happy to book.

Why it fits: special and different. Caveat: it breaks the coastal tuna theme, so use only if repetition sets in.

Booking level: high.

Casa Manteca Cadiz institution

Casa Manteca is the opposite of a polished tasting menu: standing, crowded, characterful and built for small plates. It is valuable because it gives Cadiz texture. Use it as part of a La Vina crawl, not as the only meal of the day.

Why it fits: zero fuss, high character, good contrast. Caveat: go early or accept the crowd.

Booking level: low.

La Tabernita Tiny tapas stop in La Vina

La Tabernita is useful for a compact Cadiz tapas night. It is small and simple, so the value is in using it as one stop among several rather than expecting a long, seated destination meal.

Why it fits: low planning and local scale. Caveat: tiny places can be full; keep a crawl mindset.

Booking level: low.

Activities deep dive

Places, walks and how to use them

Seville

Real Alcazar Worth doing, but do it well

The Alcazar is touristy because it is genuinely exceptional. The way to make it fit your trip is to avoid a tired midday visit: book early or late, move slowly through the gardens, and treat the site as a layered palace rather than a checklist item.

Why it fits: architecture, shade, water, planting and history in one place. Useful note: official hours are published by the Alcazar and May normally has long daytime opening.

Casa de Pilatos High-value palace

Casa de Pilatos is one of the best Seville choices for your taste: rich architecture, patios, tiles and Renaissance-Mudejar detail without the same visitor intensity as the Alcazar. It is compact enough to enjoy without derailing the day.

Why it fits: beautiful, distinctive and efficient. Use it on 4 or 5 May if you want architecture without more queue stress.

Palacio de las Duenas Lived-in aristocratic Seville

Duenas gives a different palace mood: more lived-in, domestic and garden-led. It is especially good if you want patios and history without committing to another massive monument.

Why it fits: distinctive but low burden. May opening runs into the evening according to published visitor information, which makes it useful after a long lunch.

Triana ceramics and river walk Craft, neighbourhood and light

Triana works because it is not just a museum visit. The ceramics centre adds craft context, then the river and neighbourhood give a relaxed wander that is easy to abandon or extend.

Why it fits: local texture, architecture-adjacent, low booking pressure. Pair with a river sunset rather than another major sight.

Hospital de la Caridad Quiet baroque intensity

This is a strong optional stop if you like art and architecture but dislike doing famous sights just because they are famous. It has a concentrated story, major Seville baroque art, and usually a quieter feel than the Cathedral zone.

Why it fits: high cultural density for a short visit. Check current hours before walking over.

Italica Optional Roman half-day

Italica is the best Seville-side add-on if you want ancient history and open-air ruins. It is not essential for this trip because Baelo Claudia can do the Roman-coast combination later, but it is worthwhile if you have a cooler morning and want to escape the city.

Why it fits: architecture and history, no beach dependency. Caveat: official Andalusian sites are generally closed Mondays.

Conil, coast and day trips

Calas de Roche Short, beautiful coastal walk

This is the best first coastal walk from Conil: accessible, scenic and easy to scale. The value is cliffs, coves, pines and sea colour without spending the day in the car.

Use it when: you want activity but not a mission. Avoid exposed hours if the sun or wind is hard.

Fuente del Gallo and El Roqueo Easy sunset rhythm

This is the low-admin Conil move: beach, cliffs and sunset without a formal hike. It is perfect on arrival day or after a tuna lunch when you need movement but not effort.

Use it when: you want the day to feel coastal without driving. Check tide and wind before committing to a long beach walk.

La Brena and Torre del Tajo Best hike-food pairing

La Brena is the strongest hike in the plan: pine forest, cliff views and a clear endpoint at Torre del Tajo. It pairs naturally with Barbate and El Campero, which turns the day into a complete coast-and-tuna experience.

Use it when: you want one proper active morning. Keep lunch booked after the walk so the day has a relaxed structure.

Cabo Trafalgar, Zahora and Los Canos Lighthouse and beach drift

This area is best as a late-day coastal module: lighthouse, dunes, low walks, sunset and a looser beach-bar mood. It is atmospheric rather than demanding, which makes it useful after Barbate or Vejer.

Use it when: you need a flexible afternoon. Caveat: wind can define the whole experience, so keep expectations weather-led.

Vejer de la Frontera White-town architecture and lunch

Vejer is the best town visit from Conil. It gives hilltop views, white lanes, architecture and a good food scene in a compact visit. It works especially well as the 10 May transfer stop to Cadiz.

Use it when: you want beauty and history without a museum day. Park outside the tight old core and walk up.

El Palmar Wide beach and surf mood

El Palmar is the beach to use when you want space, sand and a less town-centred day. It is not a cultural stop; it is there to make the trip feel like a holiday.

Use it when: you want to do less. Pair with Conil's tuna route later rather than over-planning the day.

Cadiz old town Best final base if you move

Cadiz is the best solution to the 10 to 13 May question because it adds variety without adding a hard itinerary. It is naturally walkable, good in mixed weather, and has enough food, sea walls, Roman remains and neighbourhoods to fill time without planning.

Use it when: you want less car and more wandering. Park once and treat the car as airport equipment.

Bolonia and Baelo Claudia Best long day trip

Bolonia is the one longer trip that most justifies itself: a Roman archaeological site, a wild beach and the dune landscape in one day. It is a better fit than many famous Andalusian day trips because it combines history and coast.

Use it when: weather is clear and you are ready for the longer drive. Caveat: Baelo Claudia follows Andalusian monument schedules, so avoid Monday and check hours.

Medina Sidonia Cloudy-day hill town

Medina Sidonia is a good substitute if wind makes beach days less appealing. It is a historic hill town with walls, arches and views, and it asks less of you than a major city day.

Use it when: you want an inland change without committing to Jerez or another big plan.

Weather-led swaps

  • Hot Seville day: palaces early, long lunch, Triana or Duenas late.
  • Strong coastal wind: Cadiz, Vejer, Medina Sidonia or a long lunch beats forcing a beach day.
  • Clear calm day: Roche, La Brena or Bolonia are the best landscape choices.
  • Rain risk: Cadiz, Seville interiors, long meals and short old-town walks carry the day.

Logistics deep dive

How to keep the trip easy

Arrival in Seville

Do not collect the car on arrival. Seville is poor value with a car and airport-to-city transfer is straightforward by taxi or EA bus. If your flight lands around early evening local time, protect the night by keeping dinner flexible.

Car pickup

The cleanest plan is a 6 May pickup that matches your return location. Airport pickup and airport return is often simplest, while a city pickup can save time only if the one-way return terms are clear.

Driving rhythm

Seville airport to Conil is roughly 160 km and about 1.5 to 1.75 hours in clean conditions. Conil to Barbate is easy, Zahara is manageable, and Cadiz is close enough to make the final base feel sensible rather than like another big trip.

Parking

Reserve Alzocaire parking if possible. In Cadiz, make parking part of the hotel decision: book a hotel garage or know the public garage before arrival. In Vejer, park outside the old core and walk up.

Return morning

From Cadiz, leave around 06:45 to 07:00 for an 11:05 Seville flight. From Conil, leave around 06:20 to 06:30. Fuel the previous evening if practical and keep rental return instructions offline.

Weather

Early May is attractive because Seville is warm and the coast is usually comfortable, but wind matters on the Costa de la Luz. Treat forecasts beyond a few days as directional only and decide beach versus town plans each morning.

Considered but discounted

Good options that are not the best fit

All 10 to 13 May in Conil

Discounted as the main recommendation because four earlier nights already give Conil enough time. Keep it as the low-logistics backup if you are deeply enjoying the town and the tuna route.

Zahara or Vejer overnight

Both are tempting, but one more hotel move adds friction. Zahara is better as a 12 May tuna day. Vejer is better as a transfer or lunch stop because parking and luggage inside the old town can be annoying.

Final airport night

Useful only if you are very flight-anxious. It solves one morning at the cost of the final coastal or Cadiz evening.

Jerez

Excellent for sherry, horses and bodegas, but it asks for bookings and pulls the trip inland. It is lower fit than Cadiz, Vejer or Bolonia for your beaches, food, walks and architecture brief.

Ronda, Caminito del Rey, Donana, Gibraltar or Morocco

These are all too much for the desired pace. They would turn a relaxed tuna-and-coast trip into a logistics trip.

Aponiente or Sanlucar side trip

Aponiente is genuinely special, and Sanlucar is a great food town, but they pull focus away from almadraba country and add a major detour. Save them for a different Cadiz province food trip.

Codigo de Barra

A strong Cadiz fine-dining option, but the likely open days do not align cleanly with a Sunday to Wednesday Cadiz stay before an 11:05 flight. Keep it only if current opening times unexpectedly work.

Cathedral and Plaza de Espana as anchors

Both are worth seeing, but they should be pass-through moments rather than the centre of your Seville plan. The palaces, Triana and quieter baroque stops fit your taste better.
